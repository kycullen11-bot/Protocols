What buyback actually does

Months in forbearance count toward nothing. Buyback lets you pay for them so they count as qualifying payments.

It never takes you past 120. It fills the holes so you reach 120 sooner.

The price is the smaller of your IDR bill right before or right after the gap, whenever you file. For Kyle that's about $583 a month, locked in. Every month bought instead of paid at ~$795 saves about $212 and moves forgiveness up a month.

Two eligibility tests

  • 120 months of qualifying employment, already certified. Not 120 payments.
  • The bought months must finish you at 120. You can't use it to shrink a gap you'd still have to finish by paying.

Kyle today: 83 + 16 = 99. Fails both. Filing now gets denied.

How to file it (May 2028)

StudentAid.gov → PSLF Reconsideration → reason "PSLF Buyback." Include this language, or it can be treated as a general question:

I have at least 120 months of approved qualifying employment, and I am seeking PSLF discharge through PSLF buyback. Please assess my eligibility for PSLF buyback.

File once. Duplicates get removed, not sped up. Certify all employment first, because a new PSLF form after the agreement is issued voids it.

Do you keep paying while it's pending?

Yes. The Department's position is that you continue regular payments until the review is complete. Stopping early complicates your account.

Those payments count toward your 120 on their own, so they aren't wasted.

Do you get refunded for payments past 120?

Yes. Once the buyback is finalized and the balance hits zero, payments made after the 120th qualifying payment are refunded on Direct Loans.

Two caveats: if you have other outstanding federal loans, the surplus is applied there first and only the remainder is refunded. And refunds can be offset if you owe another federal debt.

How forgiveness actually lands

Approval, then a buyback agreement with the amount owed, then you pay within 90 days, then discharge posts. Borrowers commonly wait several weeks between paying and seeing the discharge.

There's no status page. Updates come by email only. Keep your StudentAid.gov address current and check spam.

How each formula works

New IBR. Take AGI, subtract the protected amount for your family size, take 10%, divide by 12. Never more than the 10-year Standard payment. If both spouses have loans and file jointly, the household figure splits by each person's share of the total balance.

PAYE. Same formula, but the cap is the 10-year Standard amount on the balance when you entered PAYE. That's why Kyle's is $795.

RAP. A percentage of your full AGI with no protected amount: $120/year if AGI is $10,000 or less, then 1% per $10,000 band, reaching 10% above $100,000. Divide by 12, subtract $50 per dependent, floor of $10. No standard-payment cap.

Buyback. Opens when your tracker plus your ineligible months reaches 120. Months you still need when the decision arrives, times the price per month.

Do not do these

Ways to lose money here

  • Don't consolidate or take out new federal loans. Either one on or after July 1, 2026 makes RAP your only income-driven option, and consolidation reweights PSLF counts.
  • Don't use pay stubs, for either of you. Alternative documentation is for income that dropped. It builds from gross pay and drops the deductions that make AGI lower. Picking a slow-overtime month to stand in for a year that averages ~$60k of overtime would misstate income. Kyle is capped anyway.
  • Don't check "separated or unable to access spouse's income." It doesn't apply. The form is certified under penalty of perjury.
  • Don't round income down. There's no estimation allowance. Falsification is punishable under 18 U.S.C. § 1001 by fine, up to five years, or both.
  • Kyle: don't switch plans or try to cancel the pending recertification. $795 is your PAYE ceiling. IBR now would likely cap higher on today's balance, and any new application risks another processing forbearance.
  • Don't let an IDR application sit past 60 days. Processing forbearance counts for 60 days, then turns into general forbearance, which doesn't. Call at day 55.
  • Don't file buyback early or twice. Before both tests pass it's denied. Duplicates get removed, not sped up.
  • Don't file a new PSLF form after a buyback agreement is issued. It voids the agreement, as does paying the loan off or applying to consolidate. Money already paid toward a voided agreement isn't refunded, just credited to the loans.

Good-faith estimate vs. understating

Good-faith estimate: income is genuinely variable or unknowable, and you project it using a method you could explain to a servicer.

Understating: you know the pattern and you pick a smaller number or a flattering slice of it. That's a false statement on a federal form.

Neither applies here. Both of you file W-2 returns and the tax return supplies the figure directly.

Where things stand in the program

  • SAVE was vacated March 10, 2026 and is gone.
  • RAP launched July 1, 2026. PAYE and ICR are closed to new borrowers and end entirely July 1, 2028.
  • IBR remains open to borrowers with older loans, and the partial financial hardship requirement was removed.
  • Since March 2026, buyback for SAVE-forbearance months can't be priced with the SAVE formula.
  • PSLF forgiveness remains federally tax-free. Non-PSLF forgiveness became taxable again after December 31, 2025.
  • Backlogs as of April 30, 2026: about 530,000 pending IDR applications and about 88,000 buyback requests.
