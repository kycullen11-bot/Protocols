What buyback actually does

Months in forbearance are dead calendar time — they count toward nothing. Buyback lets you purchase those months and convert them into qualifying payments.

It does not add anything past 120. It fills the holes so you reach 120 sooner.

The cost is roughly what you would have paid during those months. So the benefit isn't money — it's finishing earlier and stopping interest.

The eligibility rule people misread

Buyback requires 120 months of qualifying employment — not 120 payments. Those are different numbers, and that's the whole point. You keep working, your employment clock keeps running, and when it hits 120 you buy back the gap instead of grinding out more payments.

Timing

There is no official processing time. The Department has never published one, and said in April 2026 it had no estimate to give. If a rep quotes 45 or 90 business days, that number isn't in any guidance.

Paying for it

You do not have to pay it all in one transaction. Multiple payments are allowed, as long as the full amount reaches the servicer within 90 days of the date the agreement was sent — not the date you opened it.

So ~$8,200 could be three payments of ~$2,730. Miss the window and the agreement is void; you'd restart at the back of a 20+ month queue.

The queue works in your favor: you likely won't see an agreement for many months. Setting aside ~$600/month starting now covers most of it before the clock ever opens.

Do you keep paying while it's pending?

Yes. The Department's position is that you continue regular payments until the review is complete. Stopping early complicates your account.

Those payments count toward your 120 on their own, so they aren't wasted.

Do you get refunded for payments past 120?

Yes. Once the buyback is finalized and the balance hits zero, payments made after the 120th qualifying payment are refunded on Direct Loans.

Two caveats: if you have other outstanding federal loans, the surplus is applied there first and only the remainder is refunded. And refunds can be offset if you owe another federal debt.

How forgiveness actually lands

Approval, then a buyback agreement with the amount owed, then you pay the lump sum within 90 days, then discharge posts. Borrowers commonly wait several weeks between paying and seeing the discharge.

There's no status page. Updates come by email only — keep your StudentAid.gov address current and check spam.

How each formula works

New IBR. Take AGI, subtract the protected amount for your family size, take 10%, divide by 12. Never more than the 10-year Standard payment. If both spouses have loans and file jointly, the household figure splits by each person's share of the total balance.

RAP. A percentage of your full AGI with no protected amount: $120/year if AGI is $10,000 or less, then 1% per $10,000 band, reaching 10% above $100,000. Divide by 12, subtract $50 per dependent, floor of $10. No standard-payment cap.

Do not do these

Seven ways to lose money here

  • Don't consolidate her loans. A consolidation disbursed on or after July 1, 2026 makes RAP her only option and reweights her PSLF count.
  • Don't submit pay stubs. They build from taxable gross and would raise her payment above what the IRS figure produces.
  • Don't check "separated or unable to access spouse's income." It doesn't apply. The form is certified under penalty of perjury.
  • Don't round income down. There's no estimation allowance. Falsification is punishable under 18 U.S.C. § 1001 by fine, up to five years, or both — and separate filing already gets her near zero legitimately, so there's nothing to gain.
  • Kyle: don't switch plans or volunteer new income. It forces recertification and your payment jumps.
  • Don't file a new PSLF form after a buyback agreement is issued. It voids the agreement — as does paying the loan off or applying to consolidate. Money already paid toward a voided agreement isn't refunded, just credited to the loans.
  • Don't let an IDR application sit unprocessed. If the 90-day clock runs out, she lands on Standard and stops earning PSLF credit.

Good-faith estimate vs. understating

Worth keeping straight, since the line gets blurry in online advice.

Good-faith estimate: income is genuinely variable or unknowable, and you project it using a method you could explain to a servicer.

Understating: you know the number and you write a smaller one. That's a false statement on a federal form.

Neither is relevant to Alyssa — she's a salaried teacher with a W-2 and the IRS tool supplies the figure directly.

Where things stand in the program

  • SAVE was vacated March 10, 2026 and is gone.
  • RAP launched July 1, 2026. PAYE and ICR closed to new enrollment the same day and end entirely July 1, 2028.
  • IBR is the only legacy income-driven plan still accepting new enrollees, and the partial financial hardship requirement was removed.
  • PSLF forgiveness remains federally tax-free. Non-PSLF forgiveness became taxable again after December 31, 2025.
  • The IDR application backlog was over 576,000 as of February 2026.
